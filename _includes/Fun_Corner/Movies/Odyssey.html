As an adventure film, I highly enjoyed it. Christopher Nolan once again has the cinematography on top. The practical effects as opposed to using CGI really paid off. One of my favorite depictions were of Polyphemus, changing his appearance from a normal, stereotypical cyclops to a disfigured, warped face giant. It really adds to the "realism" and creativity of the movie. Another creative detail were the soldiers during the Hades scene actually being buried in the black sand beaches of Iceland, and forced perspective from the Laestrygonians (played by 7-ft stunt actors) fighting the crew (played by a crew of under 5-ft stunt BUFF WOMEN). Now, I actually enjoyed the acting, more so from the side cast like Helen of Troy (Lupita Nyong'o) and Sinon (Elliot Page). Anne Hathaway's scene with Tom Holland was really good, because for me it really depicts the dormant female rage that Penelope probably kept for all these years. Of course she's tired of all this BS!! Now, is it a faithful adaptation? No, but I didn't expect it to be. A bit sad "Nobody" didn't make the cut, and yes, Nolan did focus on Odysseus' guilt more than his cleverful tactics, and flattened his character dimensionally. In the myths, I think bro does not even view his conquest with regret. I think Nolan was driving the movie towards a modern audience, in a modern world, where we start questioning the morality and meaningfulness of certain "military conquests." He changed the ending, he changed the angle and directive of the Odyssey, but that's where I can accept his interpretation. Music is always awesome as always, recreating ancient Greek instruments is a whole other dedication... some might argue Well, why didn't this much effort be put in casting and costume design? Uhhh aesthetics. They probably considered it, then decided against it because of marketing. Same with cast. Obv, if you see Zendaya on the poster, you will draw in that much more watchers. Unfortunate reality, although having no one of Greek descent there is kinda questionable.
